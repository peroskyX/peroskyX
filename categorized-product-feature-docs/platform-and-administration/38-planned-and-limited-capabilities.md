# Visible but not yet generally available

**Status:** Planned, connection-limited, or incomplete  
**Primary users:** Product, support, sales, and engineering teams setting accurate expectations

## Purpose

Some capabilities are visible in the product catalogue or settings before their end-to-end workflow is ready. Keeping them here prevents an interface stub or partial implementation from being mistaken for a shipped feature.

## Capabilities explicitly described as not ready

### Additional lead destinations

Field-mapping concepts exist for **HubSpot, Salesforce, Brevo, and Google Sheets**, but the public catalogue says the connections are still being finished. Email, webhook, and Notion delivery are the available alternatives.

### Performance budgets

Settings exist for weight and speed ceilings, and public help/settings routes are present, but enforcement is described as being rebuilt. Do not promise that configured limits will currently monitor or block every page as a completed feature.

### Rule-based personalization

The planned capability would show a different section based on who is reading. This is broader than today’s deliberate account-page generation and publication workflow; the two should not be represented as equivalent.

### Always-on optimization

Automatic promotion of winning sections and retirement of weak ones is planned. Current A/B testing with Takes still requires an explicit experiment and winner decision.

### Public API access

Driving Episode from customer-owned systems is listed as planned. Internal application APIs and webhooks for form delivery do not constitute a generally available customer automation API.

## How to communicate these items

- Use “planned,” “limited,” or the exact current state rather than “available.”
- Do not attach plan entitlements or contractual commitments until the feature is launched.
- Direct users to the currently supported alternative when one exists.
- Re-audit UI, API behavior, permissions, documentation, and billing before moving an item into its own available-feature file.

# Goals, funnels, forms, and heatmaps

**Status:** Goals are available on paid analytics plans; funnels and heatmaps are Pro capabilities  
**Primary users:** Conversion, demand generation, and product-marketing teams

## Purpose

Conversion and behavior reporting explains what visitors did after arriving. Teams define meaningful outcomes, inspect where a journey loses people, and examine interaction patterns on the published page.

## Capabilities

- Define goals for a form submission, button click, link click, URL visit, or supported external action.
- Report conversion totals and conversion rates against those goals.
- Build or inspect step-based funnels and identify drop-off between steps.
- Analyze form exposure, starts, validation errors, and successful submissions.
- Compare form errors per start and identify field-level drop-off.
- Use heatmaps to see click concentration and scroll depth on the published design.
- Apply the analytics date range and relevant audience/acquisition filters.

## How it works

1. The team decides which visitor action represents success and configures the matching goal.
2. Published-page events are evaluated against that definition.
3. Goal reports show how often the outcome occurred and the share of relevant visits that converted.
4. A funnel arranges several actions into an expected order and calculates where participation falls between steps.
5. Form and heatmap views provide more specific evidence about friction or attention.

## Important behavior

A goal definition changes how events are interpreted; it does not retroactively create behavior that was never recorded. URL and click rules should be specific enough to avoid counting unrelated actions.

Heatmaps describe aggregate interaction with the page as published during the observed period. If the design changes substantially, old and new behavior may not be directly comparable. Form errors can indicate unclear validation or difficult fields, while abandonment alone does not prove the field caused the exit.

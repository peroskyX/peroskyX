# Website analytics

**Status:** Available on paid plans; Free has no analytics  
**Primary users:** Marketers, growth teams, and website owners

## Purpose

Episode analytics are attached to the published page, so teams can understand traffic and performance without installing a separate tag. The reporting surface connects acquisition, content performance, audience, goals, forms, accounts, and optimization views.

## Capabilities

- View visits and performance over a selected date range.
- Break acquisition down by channel, referrer, campaign, UTM source, medium, term, campaign name, and campaign ID where data exists.
- Identify most-visited and best-converting pages.
- Compare new and returning visitors.
- Break audience activity down by geography, device, and browser-related context.
- Filter reporting by page, device, geography, source, medium, channel, campaign, account, or segment where supported.
- Focus reporting on an experiment or account context.
- Navigate between acquisition, pages, audience, goals, forms, and optimization views.

## How it works

Once a page is published and receives traffic, Episode records the supported events and makes them available in the Episode’s analytics area. The user selects the relevant date window and applies filters to narrow the population. Report rows and summary metrics update to describe that selection.

Acquisition answers how visitors arrived. Page reports show what content they consumed and which pages convert. Audience views describe broad visitor characteristics rather than identifying every individual.

## Access and interpretation

Core analytics start on the first paid plan. More advanced capabilities such as funnels, heatmaps, and exports require Pro according to the public catalogue. Empty data can mean no matching traffic, a newly published page, an overly narrow filter, or a plan restriction.

Attribution fields depend on the information supplied by browsers and links. Missing or privacy-limited data should be treated as unknown rather than forced into an inaccurate source.
